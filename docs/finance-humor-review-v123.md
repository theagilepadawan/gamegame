# Finance humour review — complete current obstacle inventory

Approved review, implemented in version 1.24. The tables preserve the original
before/after decisions. See [the implementation](obstacles-v124.md) for the new
art, sizes, lettering, motion and occasional ticker delivery.

## Direction

The joke should be recognisable to someone who works at the fund. Target poor
judgement, hidden costs, overconfidence, needless process and incentives that
reward the wrong thing. Debt, capex, cash, bonuses, pay rises, benchmarks and
rate cuts are not inherently bad. Nor is ordinary volatility necessarily a
reason for a long-term investor to run away.

An obstacle can still be a neutral physical pun: clearing a **Hurdle Rate** and
beating a **Benchmark** work because the action explains the joke. Make those
playful challenges, not cautionary messages about good investment practice.

The tables cover all **35 distinct active obstacle captions**, across all three
Story chapters and all five Endless themes. Shared labels are listed once;
the places where they recur are noted. They are read from the actual authored
level objects and the Endless theme definitions, not a list of unused strings.

## Supporting copy and scenery

The renderer's unused generic fallback labels are MARKET RISK and ENTRY FEE.
If a future object needs a fallback, use **HIDDEN RISK** and **FEE ON FEES**;
do not silently introduce an extra unrelated joke.

Keep the positive pickup vocabulary: **Dividend, Opportunity, Diversification,
Research, Conviction, Contrarian** and the **IPO jetpack**. Keep their names
separate from hazard vocabulary. Contrarian's existing “Everyone agrees.
Nicolai has questions.” is characterful; it does not need a claim that disagreeing
always makes money. The jetpack remains playful IPO spectacle.

Keep the useful route and location signs: **High Conviction / Street Level,
Fifth Avenue, Work in Progress, Canopy Walk / Lakeside Path, Bethesda Terrace,
Take the Long View**. Keep the secondary wayfinding **Upper Route / Lower Route,
Onward, Together, Let It Compound, Growth Takes Work** and the platform detail
**A Little Upside**. **LIFT 02** is plausible industrial dressing and need not
carry another joke. **Topping Out** remains the authored Midtown finish marker;
the current finish artwork presents 505. Do not reintroduce a second office.

Keep the actual destination lettering **Union Square**, **505 FIFTH**, **The
Long View** and **Returns, with a View**. These identify places and chapter
completion; forcing a punchline into every sign would weaken the world.

## What would make this succeed

1. **Let the object finish the joke.** A benchmark plaque, a creeping invoice,
   a moving forecast and a bag left on a bench are more memorable than a new
   finance term printed on the same rectangle. Reuse the approved warm art
   direction and material palette; do not swap every asset in this copy pass.
2. **Keep the action instantly legible.** Two to three words, one clear solid
   edge, generous letter contrast. Longer phrases use two lines. Test at the
   actual smallest phone size; do not squash text to make it fit.
3. **Use dry follow-up lines where there is time to read.** Suggested occasional
   ticker/result lines: “On budget. On the new budget.” (Capex Creep), “All
   independent thinkers. Same position.” (Crowded Trade), “Net of fees. Then
   net of more fees.” (Fee on Fees), and “Due diligence: everyone else bought
   it.” (FOMO). Do not plaster subtitles onto fast-moving obstacles.
4. **Build a few earned running jokes.** FOMO → More FOMO, a genuinely moving
   guidance board, and the second final question. Use a recent-history buffer
   in Endless so these remain occasional rather than every twenty seconds.
   Joke scheduling must still respect jump/slide spacing and solvable routes.
5. **Keep each district's character.** Downtown: pricing, expectations and
   crowded trades. Midtown: fees, budgets, forecasts and excessive process.
   Park: benches, branches, patience and perspective. Most objects should still
   look like they belong in that street or park.
6. **Punch up and keep the warmth.** Satirise overconfidence and self-serving
   systems, not workers' salaries or the fund's ordinary investment tools.
   Leave some signs sincere. The long-term-investing message should survive
   the joke.
7. **Review in motion with the audience.** A short run-through with a few fund
   colleagues should test recognition, readability and whether the gag lands
   without explanation. Cut the weaker half of any extra joke pool rather
   than filling the game with terminology.
